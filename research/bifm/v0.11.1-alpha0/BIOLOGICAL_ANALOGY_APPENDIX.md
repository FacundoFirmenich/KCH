# Appendix — Biological analogy, biomimetic contracts and claim ceilings

## A. The category problem

Modern learned systems are statistical-computational software systems embedded in technical-statistical-computational configurations. Biological intelligence is a property of living, embodied, developmental and socially coupled organisms. Shared observable functions do not place the two classes on a single total order.

The following relations must remain distinct:

`biological homology != functional analogy != mechanistic simulation != organizational equivalence != phenomenal equivalence`.

A neural-network component may be historically inspired by a biological concept without reproducing the biological mechanism. Terms such as neuron, attention, memory, learning, reward and agent are often useful technical abstractions; they do not transfer all biological or psychological properties of their source terms.

## B. Biomimetic contract

Every biological inspiration claim should carry a contract

`B = (S, I, A, O, V, F)`

where:

- `S`: biological source system;
- `I`: invariant or organizational property intended to be preserved;
- `A`: artificial abstraction or implementation;
- `O`: omitted biological mechanisms;
- `V`: validation domain and measurement operator;
- `F`: conditions under which the analogy fails.

Example:

- source: cortical recurrent processing;
- invariant: iterative state refinement under feedback;
- abstraction: recurrent/iterative neural computation;
- omissions: metabolism, neuromodulation, glia, development, body coupling;
- validation: task-relative temporal dynamics;
- failure: no improvement or no corresponding dynamical signature under intervention.

The contract licenses only the declared invariant, not organismic equivalence.

## C. Biological organization vector

A theory-dependent biological profile may include:

`b = (boundary, metabolism, homeostasis, repair, reproduction, development, sensorimotor coupling, viability regulation, intrinsic teleology, norm production)`.

No single universal score is assumed. Different theories may require different necessary or sufficient conditions.

An SSEC can simulate, predict, or control one of these properties without materially realizing it. A CTEC can supply an external infrastructure that implements part of a function without locating the function inside the model artifact.

Thus:

`simulated function != realized mechanism != constitutive organization`.

## D. Embodiment and environment

Human intelligence is not simply

`brain -> computation`.

It arises through coupled dynamics among nervous system, body, metabolism, endocrine and immune regulation, sensory-motor activity, development, culture and environment.

A software system can be sensorimotor and embodied in a weak engineering sense while lacking organismic embodiment. The term *embodied* must therefore specify:

- physical boundary;
- energetic dependence;
- material self-maintenance;
- sensor and effector coupling;
- developmental history;
- viability constraints;
- locus of control and repair.

## E. Autonomy vector

Autonomy is represented as a vector rather than a binary label:

`a = (operational, energetic, material, maintenance, reproductive, developmental, teleological, normative)`.

High operational autonomy does not imply energetic, material, teleological or normative autonomy.

An agentic software loop may plan and execute many steps while remaining externally dependent for:

- goals;
- compute;
- energy;
- maintenance;
- identity;
- permission;
- authority;
- legal and moral responsibility.

## F. Genealogy and performance

Let `A triangleleft_g H` mean that an artifact is genealogically derived from human technoculture. Let `A >_D H` mean that the artifact has higher performance in a declared domain.

Both can hold simultaneously:

`A triangleleft_g H and A >_D H`.

Performance does not invert genealogy. Genealogy does not prevent local super-capability. Neither relation determines biological organization, consciousness or authority.

Technology in the broad sense predates Homo sapiens and is not unique to humans. The relevant historical claim is narrower: contemporary AI and the digital technosphere depend on a human technocultural genealogy.

## G. Inversion without biological superiority

A CTEC can become causally dominant in a local institutional process without becoming biologically or ontologically superior.

Instrumental inversion depends on variables such as:

- access to resources;
- delegated authority;
- operational speed;
- opacity;
- replication;
- override latency;
- institutional concentration;
- reversibility.

Therefore:

`loss of control does not imply consciousness or biological superiority`.

And:

`human derivation does not imply effective human control`.

## H. Behavioral evidence and biological claims

Let `O_B` be a behavioral observation and `q_bio` a biological target property.

The biological claim is identifiable from behavior only if:

`O_B(m1)=O_B(m2) => q_bio(m1)=q_bio(m2)`

within a declared admissible class.

If a collision witness exists, the evidence does not identify the biological property. This does not prove that the property is absent. It proves that the given evidence is insufficient.

The same distinction applies to consciousness:

`behavioral evidence does not by itself identify phenomenal state`.

This is not equivalent to:

`artificial consciousness is impossible`.

## I. Brain-model comparison

A model may predict neural recordings, align representational geometry with a brain region, or select stimuli that alter measured brain activity. Such evidence can establish:

- predictive alignment;
- representational similarity;
- intervention-relative correspondence;
- local functional analogy.

It does not automatically establish:

- identical computation;
- identical causal organization;
- biological homology;
- organismic equivalence;
- shared experience.

Each stronger claim requires a stronger bridge and a more informative observation/intervention family.

## J. Language and mental-state attribution

Language generation can support claims about linguistic competence under declared tests. It does not automatically identify beliefs, desires, understanding, self-awareness or consciousness.

Mental-state vocabulary should be typed:

- operational shorthand;
- behavioral disposition;
- functional role;
- representational hypothesis;
- phenomenal attribution.

A benchmark called self-awareness that measures error detection establishes an error-monitoring capability unless an additional bridge identifies the stronger property.

## K. Responsibility and anthropomorphic laundering

Statements such as “the AI decided” can obscure:

- who defined objectives;
- who selected data;
- who set thresholds;
- who authorized deployment;
- who owns the infrastructure;
- who could revoke execution;
- who benefits;
- who bears responsibility.

Anthropomorphism may therefore operate as responsibility laundering. CTEC analysis retains human and institutional nodes in the causal graph instead of placing all agency in the model.

## L. Falsifiable biomimetic research programme

A biological analogy becomes scientifically useful when it predicts measurable advantages or constraints.

For every biomimetic claim preregister:

1. biological source and invariant;
2. artificial implementation;
3. omitted mechanisms;
4. matched non-biological baseline;
5. interventions distinguishing rival mechanisms;
6. task and jurisdiction;
7. metrics and uncertainty;
8. failure conditions;
9. claim ceiling.

A valid result may show that preserving one biological invariant improves robustness, sample efficiency, adaptation or control. It does not promote the system to a biological organism unless the organismic criteria are independently satisfied.

## M. Canonical formulation

> Contemporary learned AI systems are statistical-computational software systems embedded in technical-statistical-computational configurations. They can exhibit extraordinary local capabilities, operational autonomy and causal influence. Those properties do not by themselves establish biological organization, mechanistic identity with brains, phenomenal consciousness, intrinsic normativity or legitimate authority. Every ascent requires an identifying bridge specific to the target property.

Compactly:

`performance_D does not imply mechanism equivalence does not imply biological equivalence does not imply phenomenal equivalence does not imply normative authority`.

The arrows may be crossed only by declared assumptions, observations, interventions, falsifiers and certificates.
