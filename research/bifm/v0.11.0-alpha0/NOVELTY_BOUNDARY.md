# Novelty boundary — BIFM v0.11

## Not claimed as new

BIFM does not claim novelty for:

- identifiability as constancy on observation fibers;
- factorization through an observation map;
- Blackwell/Le Cam comparison of statistical experiments;
- monotonic information gain under sufficient observation refinement;
- Beta–Binomial hierarchical pooling;
- expected information gain as a Bayesian design criterion;
- non-identifiability of steering interventions in isolation;
- sensitivity of multiple-choice models to answer order.

## Direct contemporary adjacency

Recent work on steering-vector identifiability demonstrates large equivalence classes of behaviorally indistinguishable interventions and recovery under additional structural assumptions. Recent mechanistic-interpretability position work argues that validation metrics do not by themselves identify causal claims.

BIFM therefore does not claim that intervention non-identifiability or identification-assumption disclosure was first introduced here.

## Residual contribution targeted by the integrated programme

The contribution under test is the composition of:

1. typed claim levels spanning statistical, behavioral, mechanistic, biological, phenomenal, causal, genealogical, control and authority claims;
2. explicit admissible system classes and observation operators;
3. machine-checkable positive factorization certificates and negative collision witnesses;
4. fail-closed `NOT_IDENTIFIABLE` outcomes;
5. cumulative observation lattices and exact contraction tests;
6. architecture-relative intervention quotients;
7. control-coordinate and realized-effect frontiers;
8. real open-weight intervention experiments;
9. jurisdictional Bayesian uncertainty without global score collapse;
10. future-only lineage and external authority separation through KCH.

Any broader priority claim remains prohibited pending a systematic literature review and citation-network search.
